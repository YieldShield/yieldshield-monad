# Metropolis narrated product overview

This video uses actual application screenshots and synthetic narration. Transaction execution is evidenced separately by confirmed testnet receipts; no live wallet signing is shown.

## 01-home

YieldShield on Monad gives crypto holders two ways to exit. Withdraw the remaining deposited asset, or surrender the position for its eligible backing payout. Another participant supplies first-loss capital and earns a share of realized gains. This narrated overview shows the deployed testnet product and its recorded transaction evidence.

## 02-markets

Five funded pools are deployed on Monad testnet. The reference markets support wrapped native MON and shMON, backed by either Test USD or redeemable test-vault shares. RedStone supplies MON pricing directly on chain. Test USD has no monetary value and is not Circle USDC. Each pool began with fifty thousand test backing units.

## 03-protect

The protection form exposes the asset, backing and gain-sharing fees before signing. There is no recurring premium. A backing exit requires a sixty-second testnet wait and surrenders the remaining asset. Dynamic email authentication and embedded wallets are integrated. Their user signing walkthrough is still being verified; this video does not present a live wallet transaction.

## 04-lab

The separate scenario lab makes gains and losses understandable without pretending a formula is a market price. Holders can compare taking the remaining asset with using the capped backing option. Junior providers accept first losses. Their one-hundred-and-twenty-second withdrawal notice does not release capital that is still committed to protection.

## 05-tokens

Test assets let visitors obtain demonstration tokens, wrap native MON and stake through the actual shMonad integration. The test vault uses deposited backing and deliberately funded test yield. Its payout is in vault shares, followed by a separate redemption. shMON uses conservative withdrawal value; staking does not mean that an unstake queue has completed.

## 06-status

The status page reads verified contracts and fresh prices through a read-only API. MON reference rounds older than one hundred and twenty seconds are rejected. Scenario prices remain isolated. The API has no wallet signing key. Users keep explicit transaction confirmations, exact token approvals and account, network and contract checks.

## 07-evidence

Both internal transaction suites completed with canonical receipts and received-token balance checks. They cover deposits, both holder exits, provider claims and actual withdrawal waits. One failed gas estimate and its successful reviewed retry remain visible. YieldShield predates Metropolis; the protocol foundation is reused and disclosed. The Monad app, integrations and deployment form the new milestone. No adoption or independent audit is claimed.
